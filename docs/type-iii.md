# Type III: statement and formalization map

The September 18 entry
[`TypeIIICoherentUniformApplicationsFromGeneralStandardTheory03.lean`](../formal/TypeIIICoherentUniformApplicationsFromGeneralStandardTheory03.lean)
derives the exact original `HasFiniteExceptionalTypeIIIInput` from general
published-theorem interfaces on one common theory. Its `computedTypeIIIInput`
theorem constructs the uniform constants and the applications to the original
family. See the [cited derivation](type-iii-published-general-application.md)
for the precise operators, diagrams, theorem inputs and cutoff.

The existence and joint continuous-adic interpretation of that common theory
remain external. The result is conditional on these explicit interfaces;
it is not a closed proof of the Type III proposition. The numerical integral
inequalities remain separate hypotheses. [STATUS.md](../STATUS.md) distinguishes
the current integration checks from prior full verification runs.

[`TypeIIILocalProgress.lean`](../formal/TypeIIILocalProgress.lean) remains the
entry for earlier supporting results. The component map below includes the
retained partial reconstruction of the étale and adic foundations.

## The unchanged target

All sums below are over the prime field. Let

```text
Kl₃(t) = p⁻¹ Σ_{u,v ≠ 0} eₚ(u+v+t/(uv)),
Cₚ(A,B,c) = Σ_{x ≠ 0} Kl₃(Ax) conjugate(Kl₃(Bx)) eₚ(cx),
Kₘₙ(r₁,r₂) = Cₚ(αr₂/(mr₁²), αr₁/(nr₂²), 1),
W = Kₘₙ conjugate(Kₘ′ₙ) Kₘ′ₙ′ conjugate(Kₘₙ′),
Ŵ(h,k) = Σ_{r₁,r₂} W(r₁,r₂)eₚ(hr₁+kr₂).
```

`K` is extended by zero when `r₁r₂=0`; so is `W`. The Fourier transform is
unnormalized and uses the positive additive-character phase.

The target is to find `C>0` and natural numbers `D,p₀` such that, for every
prime `p>p₀` and all nonzero `α,m,m′,n,n′`, both applicable conclusions hold:

- If `m≠m′` and `n≠n′`, there is `Z ⊆ Fₚ²`, with `|Z|≤D`, such that
  `|Ŵ(h,k)| ≤ C p³ (1 + √p · 1_Z(h,k))` for every frequency.
- If `m=m′` or `n=n′`, there is a nonzero plane polynomial `P` over the
  algebraic closure of `Fₚ`, of total degree at most `D`, such that
  `|Ŵ(h,k)| ≤ C p³ (1 + √p · 1_{P(h,k)=0} + p · 1_{h=k=0})`
  for every prime-field frequency.

The constants are chosen before the prime and residue parameters. The
exceptional set or polynomial may depend on them. A curve of exceptional
frequencies is insufficient in the distinct-index case. A zero polynomial,
a degree bound depending on `p`, or a prime-dependent constant does not meet
this target. The exact definitions are
[`FiniteExceptionalFourierBound`, `CurveExceptionalFourierBound`,
`LocalFourierHypothesis`, and `HasFiniteExceptionalTypeIIIInput`](../formal/TypeIIILocal.lean).

More explicitly, put `a=m/m′`, `b=n/n′`,
`P_A(B)=C(A,B) conjugate(C(aA,B))`, and
`μ_A=(p−1)⁻¹ Σ_B P_A(B)`, where the column sum is over units.
The centered coefficient replaces
`P_A(B) conjugate(P_A(bB))` by
`(P_A(B)−μ_A) conjugate(P_A(bB)−μ_A)` inside the original physical Fourier sum.
The proved `32p³` difference estimate retains the nonlinear coordinates,
all physical fibers, and the Fourier phase. It isolates an error at the
required scale; cancellation in the remaining coefficient is still needed.

## The geometric links still to formalize

The geometric route uses the following chain. Each step must refer to the
actual family underlying the finite sums above.

The Artin–Schreier cover, its locally trivial module sheaf, and its character
stalk supply the additive-character foundation, including actual geometric
Frobenius traces at finite-field points of an affine base. The construction
also works at the explicit finite torsion coefficient levels above, whose
topological inverse limit is now identified as a finite free Noetherian
`Z_ell`-algebra, complete and separated for its actual adic topology,
with its original finite quotients. The canonical extension
of coefficients along each finite reduction recovers the lower-level
sheaf. Exact extension by zero is available on the actual small étale
sites, including the proved compactification of the unchanged phase
family into the projective plane over the parameter line. The relative
derived direct image for that compactification is constructed using actual
injective resolutions. Actual inverse image is exact, and the derived
base-change morphism is constructed from the original ordinary mate and
an exact mapped-resolution comparison. Its degree-zero compatibility
with that original map is proved.
For monomorphic étale base change, the ordinary map and the same derived
map are proved invertible in every degree. Extension by zero commutes
with arbitrary inverse image. Exact global sections over a separably
closed field identify the target of the original geometric-point map
with actual cohomology on the literal fiber. For the unchanged phase
family, this is cohomology of its actual extension by zero on its proper
fiber. Its finite system, computed in the fixed limit-ring module
category, retains its original torsion exponents in every degree, and
the same geometric comparison respects the actual coefficient reductions.
What remains here is invertibility of that map for the proper projection,
the compatible adic cohomological construction, its trace formula, and
the geometric estimates.
The algebraic acyclic-resolution criterion is now proved for the same
map. Its geometric inputs remain to be discharged. Exact global sections
over a strictly henselian local base identify the source stalk with
cohomology of the whole scheme over that base. Closed-fiber detection
proves uniqueness for extending étale sections; existence and the
resulting cohomological comparison with that fiber remain to be proved.
Restriction to the actual closed fiber is now also proved injective on
global sections of every module sheaf. Finite étale isomorphisms over a
proper local base are detected on that fiber. Actual idempotent lifting
for henselian pairs and coprime-factorization lifting for henselian local
rings supply further algebraic ingredients for extension existence.
Finite-module annihilators, the lifted factorization and a Bézout identity
now prove idempotent lifting for every finite algebra over the henselian
local base. Descent of the actual finite ideal-membership witness proves
the same result for every integral algebra. The original quotient
`A → A/mA` induces an order isomorphism on clopens of spectra, with no
localness or henselianity assumption on `A`. Lifting clopens on proper
nonaffine schemes remains a further step.
For the original affinization of a proper morphism, properness,
surjectivity, integrality of the second factor and the structure-sheaf
isomorphism are proved. Nilpotent thickenings preserve actual global
idempotents, and the actual infinitesimal neighborhoods carry the unique
compatible family determined by an idempotent on the closed subscheme.
The additional proper-fiber section-image statement and its
formal-functions dependency are explained in the
[affinization note](../research/type_iii/geometry/proper_affinization_gap.md).
The canonical comparison from completed global sections to compatible
sections of the actual infinitesimal schemes is now constructed and
agrees with the original restrictions. It is a linear equivalence for
affine schemes. For general proper schemes its surjectivity remains
unproved. The separate range-descent theorem proves that a completed
preimage of the original idempotent family would give an original
section with the prescribed closed-fiber value.
Separately, the ordinary global and relative cohomology comparisons
under arbitrary restriction of coefficient rings are now proved. The
actual skyscraper products and Godement retract discharge their
acyclicity inputs. The actual coextension adjunction also gives the
coefficient comparison for extension by zero, so the original composite
`R^n barπ_*(j!F)` has this comparison with its degree-zero square. The
derived image in each actual finite coefficient category restricts to
the corresponding object of the original common-coefficient tower.
The original derived comparisons now respect composition. Consequently
the finite-category transitions, defined directly from the original
sheaf reductions, agree with the original common-coefficient tower under
those levelwise comparisons. This is an isomorphism of actual inverse
systems. The transitions also satisfy composition within the finite
coefficient categories themselves, with the original scalar-composition
isomorphism. The original coefficient short exact sequence also gives
the Bockstein long exact sequence for these finite-category derived
objects, with the original reduction and canonical connecting maps.
Its other terms are ordinary derived images of the discrete limit-ring
sheaf. The adic cohomology and inverse-limit comparisons remain to be proved.
Ordinary sheaf cohomology with discrete complex coefficients does not
supply these missing constructions.

1. **Trace realization.** Construct the normalized rank-three Kloosterman
   sheaf and the pure rank-six family whose trace is the corrected
   correlation. Prove the trace identity, cohomological concentration,
   rank and purity, including the zero-stalk correction `κₚ`.
2. **Local Fourier characters.** Compute the complete local character list
   for that family along the divisors used in the support argument. The
   checked scalar phase identities apply only after this
   representation-theoretic identification.
3. **Fourier support.** Construct the Fourier-transform compatibility with
   coefficient conjugation, linear pullbacks, and intermediate extension.
   The route using dilation by eight requires an action on the actual
   geometric Fourier supports, together with uniform bounds on their
   cardinality or degree. The checked line-cover lemmas apply once those
   hypotheses are supplied. One must then exclude the remaining line and
   punctual constituents in the distinct-index case using the actual local
   characters. The complex finite-sum covariance alone does not supply any
   of these sheaf-theoretic conclusions.
4. **Uniform bounds.** Prove the required weights, intermediate-extension
   stalk bounds, and quantitative complexity bounds for the whole parameter
   family. These must give bounded finite exceptions for distinct indices,
   and the stated curve and origin exceptions for repeated indices.
5. **Actual core estimates.** Combine those geometric results with the
   proved boundary and expansion lemmas to produce uniform witnesses for
   `HasFiniteExceptionalTypeIIIInput`.

Relevant mathematical sources include
[Fu's local Fourier formulas](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0702436),
[Laumon's Fourier and stationary-phase theory](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1987__65__131_0/),
and [Quantitative sheaf theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00635).
They are a map of the intended geometric input; citing them does not
formalize their application to this family.

The direct finite-sum identities are also available for alternative proofs.
The present scalar norm bounds give `O(p⁴)` for the full four-cycle transform;
the generic target is `O(p³)`. The centering reduction removes an `O(p³)`
error but does not supply the additional cancellation in the remainder,
including at the nonzero frequencies of the repeated-index branch.

Completion requires a compiled theorem of the unchanged target type, together
with an audit of its full mathematical premises. Defining an equivalent
hypothesis, printing it, or proving an implication from it is not completion.
