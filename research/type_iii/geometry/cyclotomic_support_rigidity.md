# Cyclotomic covariance forces the exceptional supports to be conical

This note proves a mathematical shortcut for the Type III geometric
argument. It uses the actual Kloosterman and corrected-correlation
construction, together with standard sheaf functoriality and the existing
quantitative complexity theorems. It is not a Lean proof.

For sufficiently large characteristic, the shortcut has two consequences:

1. Every curve-supported Fourier constituent is supported on a line
   through the origin.
2. Once these line constituents are excluded, the intrinsic finite
   exceptional set from ordinary degree \(-1\) cohomology is contained
   in the origin.

The second conclusion concerns geometric support, not the size of a
complex number and not an arbitrarily chosen exceptional set in a
norm inequality. The method does not assume equal absolute values for
cyclotomic conjugates.

## The actual finite-sum identity

Write \(K_{3,\psi}\), \(C_\psi\), \(W_\psi\), and
\(\widehat W_\psi\) with the normalization and positive Fourier phase
in the Type III statement. For \(a\in\mathbf F_p^\times\), let
\(\sigma_a(\zeta_p)=\zeta_p^a\) on the cyclotomic field. Scaling the two
Kloosterman summation variables gives
\[
 \sigma_a K_{3,\psi}(t)=K_{3,\psi}(a^3t).
 \tag{1}
\]
The automorphism commutes with complex conjugation on this abelian
cyclotomic field. Substituting \(y=ah\) in the correlation gives
\[
 \sigma_a C_\psi(A,B,1)
  = C_\psi(a^3A,a^3B,a)
  = C_\psi(a^2A,a^2B,1).
 \tag{2}
\]
Both physical parameters \(A_{mn}\) and \(B_{mn}\) are homogeneous
of degree \(-1\) in \((r_1,r_2)\). Thus
\[
 \sigma_a W_\psi(r_1,r_2)
       =W_\psi(a^{-2}r_1,a^{-2}r_2).
 \tag{3}
\]
Finally substitute \((r_1,r_2)=a^2(s_1,s_2)\) in the Fourier sum:
\[
 \boxed{\ \sigma_a\widehat W_\psi(h,k)
           =\widehat W_\psi(a^3h,a^3k).\ }
 \tag{4}
\]
The zero extension on the axes is respected by all substitutions.
The same formulas hold for every nonempty corrected-core subproduct,
since \(\kappa_p=1+p^{-1}+p^{-2}\) is rational.

Equation (4) alone does not prove invariance of the frequencies with
large complex absolute value. That inference would be false in general.
The additional argument below works with a sheaf isomorphism and
intrinsic support.

## A continuous coefficient automorphism suffices

Fix the auxiliary coefficient prime \(\ell=2\), for all \(p>3\).
There is a continuous automorphism
\(\sigma\in\operatorname {Gal}(\overline{\mathbf Q}_2/\mathbf Q_2)\)
whose action on the odd-order roots of unity is
\(\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^2\): take a lift of arithmetic Frobenius from
the maximal unramified extension. The local-field ingredients are
[Milne, *Algebraic Number Theory*, Proposition 7.50, Corollary 7.51
and Example 7.54](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf).

This choice is enough. It avoids the generally unjustified assumption
that every cyclotomic \(\sigma_a\) extends to a continuous automorphism
fixing an arbitrarily chosen \(\mathbf Q_\ell\). For fixed \(\ell\)
the automatically available exponents form the subgroup generated by
\(\ell\bmod p\); here we only use its element \(2\).

Coefficient conjugation by this continuous automorphism is an exact
semilinear equivalence of constructible sheaf categories. It commutes
with pullback, the two direct images, duality, ordinary and perverse
cohomology, and intermediate extension. In particular, it preserves the
dimension of every stalk and the geometric support of every ordinary
cohomology sheaf. It fixes all the integer Tate twists and the constant
sign twist used in the corrected correlation.

## Sheaf-level covariance of the actual corrected family

Use the whole-torus parabolic-cohomology construction in
[relative_core_lissity.md](relative_core_lissity.md).
Let \(\mathcal H_\psi\) denote its corrected rank-six sheaf on
\(S=\mathbf G_{m,\lambda}\times\mathbf G_{m,\xi}\).

The Kloosterman sheaf is the appropriate cohomology of the product map
from three multiplicative coordinates with additive phase their sum.
Multiplying all three coordinates by \(2\) gives the geometric
isomorphism
\[
 \sigma\mathcal K_\psi \simeq [8]^*\mathcal K_\psi.
 \tag{5}
\]
Thus this is a change-of-variables isomorphism of sheaves, not an
inference from a trace identity at \(\mathbf F_p\)-points.

In the defining relative sheaf
\[
 \mathcal F_\psi(x,\lambda,\xi)=
 \mathcal K_\psi(x)\otimes\mathcal K_\psi(\lambda x)^\vee
                         \otimes\mathcal L_\psi(\xi x),
\]
coefficient conjugation replaces the last phase by \(2\xi x\).
The change \(x\mapsto8x\) therefore gives
\[
 \sigma\mathcal F_\psi(x,\lambda,\xi)
 \simeq \mathcal F_\psi(8x,\lambda,\xi/4).
 \tag{6}
\]
This is a Cartesian change of variables above
\(b(\lambda,\xi)=(\lambda,\xi/4)\). Base change for both direct images
and exactness of pullback on their lisse images yield
\[
 \sigma\mathcal H_\psi\simeq b^*\mathcal H_\psi.
 \tag{7}
\]

Write \(s_c(r_1,r_2)=(cr_1,cr_2)\). The physical map is
\[
 \phi_{mn}(r_1,r_2)=
 \left(\frac{m r_1^3}{n r_2^3},
       \frac{m r_1^2}{\alpha r_2}\right),
\]
and \(\phi_{mn}\circ s_{1/4}=b\circ\phi_{mn}\).
Every physical entry consequently satisfies
\(\sigma\mathcal H_{mn}\simeq s_{1/4}^*\mathcal H_{mn}\).
This remains true for its conjugate representative
\(\mathcal H_{mn}^{\vee}(-1)\).

Let \(\mathcal G\) be any nonempty tensor subproduct of the four
corrected factors. It is lisse on the fixed torus \(T=\mathbf G_m^2\).
For the canonical immersion \(j:T\hookrightarrow\mathbf A^2\), set
\[
 P=j_{!*}(\mathcal G[2]),\qquad Q=\operatorname {FT}_\psi(P).
\]
The torus is stable under scalar dilation, so
\(\sigma P\simeq s_{1/4}^*P\).
In the defining plane Fourier transform, substitute \(r=4u\):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sigma Q
  &\simeq \operatorname {FT}_{\psi_2}(\sigma P)\\
  &\simeq \operatorname {FT}_{\psi_2}(s_{1/4}^*P)\\
  &\simeq s_8^*\operatorname {FT}_{\psi}(P)
   =s_8^*Q.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{8}
\]
The phase is \(2(4u\cdot\eta)=u\cdot(8\eta)\). Both plane transforms
carry shift \(2\), so there is no unaccounted shift. Change of variables
by an isomorphism introduces no degree factor or Tate twist.

The construction works with all unit parameters, repeated or distinct.
It is compatible with geometric base change to
\(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\).

## Two elementary rigidity lemmas

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(p>3\).
Fix an integer \(D\ge0\) with
\[
 p>8^D.
 \tag{9}
\]
The \(D+1\) elements \(1,8,\ldots,8^D\) are distinct in \(k\):
their integer representatives are distinct and less than \(p\).

**Finite support.** If a finite set \(Z\subset k^2\) is stable under
\(s_8\) and \(|Z|\le D\), then \(Z\subset\{(0,0)\}\). For a nonzero
vector \(z\), multiplication by \(z\) is injective on scalars, so
\(z,8z,\ldots,8^Dz\) would be \(D+1\) distinct elements of \(Z\).

**Curve support.** Suppose \(Y\subset k^2\) is a closed union of
irreducible curves, is stable under \(s_8\), and is contained in the
zero set of a nonzero polynomial \(f\) of total degree at most \(D\).
Then every irreducible component of \(Y\) is a line through the origin.

Here is a direct proof that needs neither a squarefree equation for
\(Y\) nor invariance of the whole zero set of \(f\). For \(z=(h,k)\in Y\),
form the one-variable polynomial
\[
 g_z(t)=f(th,tk).
 \tag{10}
\]
It has degree at most \(D\). All \(D+1\) distinct elements
\(1,8,\ldots,8^D\) are roots because \(Y\) is stable. Therefore
\(g_z=0\). Writing \(f=\sum_i f_i\) as its homogeneous decomposition
shows that every \(f_i(z)=0\). In particular
\(Y\subset V(f_{\rm top})\), where \(f_{\rm top}\ne0\) is the highest
homogeneous component of \(f\). A nonzero homogeneous binary polynomial
over \(k\) cuts out a finite union of origin lines (or the empty set if
it has degree zero). Each one-dimensional irreducible closed component
of \(Y\) lies in one such line and hence equals that line.

If the whole zero set \(V(f)\) is stable, the same argument additionally
proves that \(V(f)\) is a cone: \(g_z=0\) means \(tz\in V(f)\) for every
\(t\in k\). For the Fourier-support application only the weaker
containment in \(V(f_{\rm top})\) is needed.

The bound \(8^D<p\) is deliberately coarse. More generally the same
proof works whenever the scalar \(8\bmod p\) has multiplicative order
greater than \(D\); the displayed bound proves this without any
primitive-root conjecture or theorem on primes in progressions.

## Application to Fourier constituents and ordinary cohomology

By (8), coefficient conjugation and scalar pullback produce the same
object. Coefficient conjugation leaves intrinsic geometric supports
unchanged, whereas scalar pullback carries them to their inverse
scalar translates. It follows that each of these sets is \(8\)-stable:

- the union of supports of all dimension-one simple perverse
  constituents of \(Q\);
- the union of supports of its punctual simple perverse constituents;
- the support of each ordinary cohomology sheaf \(\mathcal H^i(Q)\).

For the first two bullets use all constituents, with geometric
Jordan–Hölder factors; an individual constituent need not be fixed.
Taking their union is essential.

The required bounds are provided by
[Quantitative sheaf theory, Theorems 6.15 and 6.23, Proposition 6.24,
and Proposition 7.18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.00635v4):
the fixed-degree construction bounds the complexity of \(Q\), the
sum of complexities of its simple constituents, their support degrees,
and the complexities of the ordinary cohomology sheaves.
Consequently one fixed \(D\), chosen before \(p\) and the residue
parameters, bounds both the union of curve supports and all relevant
finite supports. The ordinary-cohomology bound is necessary: rank alone
does not bound a finite exceptional set.

For \(p>8^D\), the two rigidity lemmas show that every possible curve
constituent is on an origin line, and every punctual constituent is at
the origin. This replaces the nonlinear-curve tangent projection and
stationary-phase argument. It also excludes nonzero punctual supports
without using radial-infinity tameness.

For distinct row and column indices, the already identified exhaustive
radial-zero wild characters exclude the constant sheaf, so the origin
punctual constituent is excluded as well. The angular phase
calculation excludes origin-line constituents. Specifically, every
nonempty tensor character has
\[
 \frac{\beta(z)}T,\qquad
 \beta(z)=z^{-1}\sum_\gamma c_\gamma(\gamma-z)^{3/2},
 \quad c_\gamma\ne0,\quad\gamma\ne0.
 \tag{11}
\]
If at least two \(\gamma\)'s occur, \(\beta(z)^2\notin k(z)\);
if one occurs, its square has a cubic zero at \(z=\gamma\).
Both are incompatible with \(c^2/(a+bz)\), the square forced by a
pullback from one linear form. Identifying (11) with the actual full
inertia representation is still indispensable.

Thus all constituents of \(Q\) have full support. Strict support for
an intermediate extension on a smooth surface gives
\(\mathcal H^0(Q)=0\), with \(\mathcal H^{-1}(Q)\) supported on a
finite set. This set is \(8\)-stable and uniformly bounded, so it is
contained in the origin. Its weight bound, together with the ordinary
degree \(-2\) bound, gives for a \(w\)-factor subproduct
\[
 |t_Q(h,k)|\ll p^{(w+2)/2}
       +p^{(w+3)/2}\mathbf 1_{(h,k)=(0,0)}.
 \tag{12}
\]
For the original raw four-cycle, the usual bounded-coefficient expansion
and physical-axis boundary comparison therefore give the stronger
large-characteristic form
\[
 |\widehat W(h,k)|\ll p^3
             \bigl(1+\sqrt p\,\mathbf 1_{(h,k)=(0,0)}\bigr).
 \tag{13}
\]
This deduction uses all the rank, purity, local-character and
complexity inputs above; the arithmetic covariance identity alone
does not imply it.

For repeated indices, curve constituents may remain but their supports
are origin lines. The zero-dimensional components of the support of
\(\mathcal H^{-1}(Q)\) are also \(8\)-stable and hence at the origin;
\(\mathcal H^0(Q)\) is likewise supported there. A product of equations
of the finitely many origin lines, adding an arbitrary origin line if
needed to contain the origin, is a nonzero bounded-degree homogeneous
polynomial supplying the repeated-index exceptional set.

## What remains to be formalized

The new elementary lemmas can be formalized independently. Their
application additionally needs the coefficient-conjugation equivalence
on the actual constructible complexes and the isomorphism (8).
The trace realization, complete local Fourier character list,
perverse Fourier equivalence, strict-support and weight theorems, and
uniform complexity estimates remain genuine mathematical inputs.

This shortcut reduces the geometric dependency chain; it does not
provide a theorem of the Lean target type by introducing a replacement
Fourier-bound hypothesis.
